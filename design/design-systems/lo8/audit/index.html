Why this exists

Audit

Six findings, all reproducible. The neutral ramp and the accent are in good shape and win as-is, they have reasoning, tests, and a coherent result. Everything else needed one home.

The website and the app are not the same brand

7/7 drifted

The comment at the top of style.css says the palette “matches the Loopable8 app’s neutral black.” It does not, and every website neutral carries the blue undertone the app deliberately removed.

7 of 7 website values are not pure neutral, and the site contains zero occurrences of either app anchor or either accent value.

The framing above was wrong, and the correction matters

This started as “the website drifted from a neutral app.” It did not. The app only removed the undertone from dark mode.

RampValuesNeutralTinted
App, dark13130
App, light13211 +2 to +10 blue
Website707 sampled

brand.dart says it outright, “Light mode is untouched; these are dark-mode values”, and its test asserts neutrality on the *Dark constants alone. So the tint was removed once, in one mode, and left everywhere else.

Which changes the fix. Repainting the website to match the app’s dark ramp closes two thirds of the gap and leaves the light theme sitting on the exact undertone both were supposed to shed. The real question is whether the colour-cast argument describes a theme or a mode, and it was written about a theme.

The website has no accent

Website --accent is #d0d0d8, greyscale. The app’s own note on why it moved off greyscale: it meant nothing on screen was allowed to be a colour, and the whole thing read as an instrument for measuring rather than a tool for making. Fixed in the app. Never fixed on the website.

Line 4 of the same file declares the aesthetic as “monochrome palette + warm amber micro-accent.” There is no amber anywhere in it, and the product’s accent is warm blue. Three different answers to one question.

The banned blue, hard-coded 20 times

5 reds, 5 ambers, 2 greens, 2 unsanctioned blues, 2 purples. Not carelessness, a structural gap. #3B82F6 is the exact value named in brand.dart as the one to avoid and listed under Banned as “default Material blue.”

The guard only watches half the system

brand_palette_test.dart is genuinely good. It asserts the anchors, the neutrality of every surface, the stacking order and the warmth of the accent, and it reads source files to catch hard-coded hexes, which is how it caught four stale literals in the Monaco WebView.

Two things it cannot see:

  • The semantic colours, because they were never tokens, there is nothing to compare a literal against.
  • The website, because it is a separate repo with no test runner. Every finding above would have failed on day one under the same rules the app holds itself to.

The boil does not boil

loopable8-boil-animated.svg is byte-identical to the static mark apart from its <title> text and one blank line. Nothing in any of the four logo files animates. Four files, two hard-coded stroke literals, one unimplemented animation, the same structural shape as the finding above.

diff loopable8-mark.svg loopable8-boil-animated.svg

What this changes

  1. tokens.json is the source. Nothing else declares a raw value.
  2. Codegen emits tokens.css and brand.dart from it, so the drift cannot recur.
  3. The semantic trio gets decided once. The proposed values are the most-used literal per role, and need sign-off.
  4. #3B82F6, #60A5FA, #7C3AED and #C4B5FD get replaced by the accent or by a sanctioned semantic token, whichever the call site actually meant.
  5. The hard-coded-hex test runs against both generated outputs, and grows to cover semantic values now that they exist to compare against.
