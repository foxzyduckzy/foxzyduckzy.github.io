Foundations

Colour

Dark mode is anchored on exactly two chosen values, #181818 for the pane you work in and #141414 for the chrome around it, and every other surface is a step derived from them.

The three laws

test-enforced

Every surface is pure neutral in dark mode

r === g === b. The dark scale used to carry a faint blue undertone (#0F0F13, #16161B) and it was removed on the reasoning that a tint is only invisible while everything shares it, a partly tinted ramp reads as a colour cast, which is louder than the tint was.

Dark holds, all 13 surface and text tokens are pure neutral.

Light does not, 11 of 13 values carry +2 to +10 of blue.

The scope is not an oversight in the reasoning, it is the reasoning being applied once. brand.dart says so outright: “Light mode is untouched; these are dark-mode values,” and its test asserts neutrality on the *Dark constants alone. But the argument was about a theme reading as a colour cast, and it is just as true of the light one, which carries the same undertone the marketing site carries on every surface. Either it holds for both themes or for neither. That is a decision, not a bug to fix in passing.

Chrome is darker than the work, in both themes

Panels are the material the chrome is cut from; the pane you work in sits above them. This is the one law stated for both themes and asserted for both, light mode always read that way, and dark mode had it inverted until it was swapped.

The stacking order above is dark-mode only. Light mode is not a luminance ramp at all: --hover and --selected go darker than the surface they sit on, which is correct for a light theme and the exact opposite of what dark mode does.

Exactly one thing on screen is not grey

A warm blue, deliberately not the usual UI blue. Blue has to lead, and the green-to-red gap has to stay under 0.12, the standard #3B82F6 sits at 0.28, which is what makes it read cold and corporate. Narrowing the gap pulls the hue toward indigo, where it sits against neutral grey without freezing it.
